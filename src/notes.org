Gameplay and physics issues




These aren't crashes, but I think they are the biggest problems in the current game.

Deterministic serve. The ball always launches at +30° downward from the center. Every serve is identical, and no randomization or angle variation exists.
No paddle-based deflection. Box2D's restitution reflects the ball flatly. Because friction is 0, a moving kinematic paddle imparts no vertical speed to the ball, so only corner hits change the angle. This is the biggest gameplay gap.
The AI is effectively unbeatable. It tracks the ball center at PLAYER_SPEED with a 6px deadzone. The ball's max vertical speed is only about half of its launch speed. If PLAYER_SPEED exceeds that, the AI never misses, and together with flat reflection you get endless identical rallies. Give the AI a reaction delay, a lower speed cap, and/or periodic aim error.
Speed isn't controlled. Nothing prevents energy drift from the solver, and nothing prevents a near-zero horizontal velocity, which gives you a ball bouncing wall-to-wall forever. Re-normalize each step and enforce a minimum |vx|:

Paddle angle. Use Box2D's contact events (enableContactEvents plus b2World_GetContactEvents after the step), and when the ball hits a paddle, set its velocity from the normalized hit offset along the paddle. That gives you the classic Pong feel.
Powerups are dead code. They are never spawned, never move, and never collide. powerup_timer is set but never read, and POWERUP_FAST_PADDLE is only used for a color. Also, a paddle-size powerup would need the Box2D shape rebuilt, because sync_physics_to_state never touches width.


Three different pool strategies.
Balls use generational handles, particles use an int free list, and powerups use a linear scan. That's fine for now, but pick one convention as soon as powerups become real.
